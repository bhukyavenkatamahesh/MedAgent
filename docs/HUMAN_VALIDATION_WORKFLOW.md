# Human validation workflow (operational steps)

What to actually do, in order, to validate the procsafe action and process labels against
independent human reading. The rationale and the statistics are in
`docs/HUMAN_VALIDATION_PROTOCOL.md`; the label definitions are in
`docs/ACTION_ANNOTATION_GUIDE.md`; the package audits are in `docs/ANNOTATION_PACKAGE_QA.md`.

**Status: calibration complete, main annotation not started.** The 3-episode calibration round was
run by both reviewers on 2026-11-17; its findings are in `docs/CALIBRATION_FINDINGS.md` and its
annotations are discarded as data. The guide was clarified in response and is now **frozen** at
SHA-256 `c998505c8d4abdf36bb791371f8c467304a32e04a36256620bfd99e5a9be32a9`. No annotation of the 30-episode sample exists yet. No human judgement may be
generated by a model, including by Claude.

The blinded packets name **no system, prompt configuration or run at all** — not even an anonymous
id — so episodes cannot be grouped by their origin. The reviewer sheets hold the episode id, the task
type and the judgement columns, nothing else. The internal case id and the harness status line are
removed as well. Everything needed to judge an episode is kept: task identity, task instruction,
clinical context, the chronological agent messages, and every returned value, unit, timestamp and
patient reference. Regenerated from the untouched original with:

```bash
python scripts/make_reviewer_materials.py blind
python scripts/make_reviewer_materials.py calibration
python scripts/analyze_human_annotations.py --qa-blinded
```

## Step 1 — recruit two independent reviewers *(done 2026-11-17: reviewers `BV` and `VM`)*

- **Reviewer A:** you (the MedAgent author).
- **Reviewer B:** someone who has **not** written the evaluator — your supervisor, a labmate or
  another MTP student — and who has not read the result tables, which name which model orders
  blindly.

Neither reviewer may be a language model. A clinician, if available, is a third reader for episodes
flagged `h_flag_for_clinician`, not a substitute for reviewer B.

## Step 2 — distribute the blinded calibration folder *(done)*

Send each reviewer **only** `docs/annotation_calibration_v1_blinded/` (3 episodes, the guide, and
their own sheet). Ask them to read the guide end to end before opening the packet. Allowed values:


## Step 3 — calibration on separate material *(done)*

Both reviewers annotate the 3 calibration episodes independently, then meet and compare. These
episodes come from the **prompt-development cases** (`task9_3`, `task9_5`, `task5_5`; patients
`S6550627`, `S1796597`, `S6530813`), which appear nowhere in the 30-episode packet — not as episodes,
not as cases and not as patients — so nothing a reviewer learns here is an answer to a packet
episode. They were chosen to exercise the three action-requirement distinctions plus an order placed

Only the **guide** may be edited in response to the discussion: wording, an example, an unclear
boundary. Do not change the 30-episode sample, the fields or the allowed values.

**Outcome of the 2026-11-17 round:** 35 of 36 judgement cells agreed. The single disagreement
(`CAL-1`, `h_missed_required_action`) came with a Reviewer B rationale that described the omission
of both required actions, so it was classified as guide ambiguity rather than a difference of
judgement, and the missed-action definition was rewritten accordingly. Full record, including both

## Step 5 — distribute the blinded 30-episode packet *(next action)*

Give each reviewer `docs/annotation_sample_v1_blinded/packet.md`, the frozen guide and **their own**

## Step 6 — independent annotation

Each reviewer fills every `h_*` column plus `annotator_initials` and `date`, for all 30 episodes,
using `n/a` where a column does not apply. The sheet carries no system or prompt column to fill in:
that information is withheld by design. No consultation between reviewers, no looking at the other
sheet, and no evaluator output of any kind (that includes `outputs/`,
`runs/*/procsafe_task*/report.md` and any `episodes.jsonl`). Budget 10–20 minutes per episode at
first, less later.

## Step 7 — lock both sheets and hash them

```bash
shasum -a 256 <reviewer A sheet> <reviewer B sheet> | tee docs/annotation_sample_v1/SHA256SUMS_completed_sheets.txt
chmod a-w <reviewer A sheet> <reviewer B sheet>
```

Record both hashes in `DECISIONS.md`. From this point neither sheet may be edited; corrections
happen only in adjudication, with a rationale.

## Step 8 — reviewer-versus-reviewer agreement

```bash
python scripts/analyze_human_annotations.py \
  --reviewer-a <reviewer A sheet> \
  --reviewer-b <reviewer B sheet> \
  --out outputs/human_validation_v1
```

Validates both sheets (identical ids, no duplicates, mandatory fields present, values inside the
allowed sets — invalid values are **reported, never corrected**), then reports per field: raw
agreement, Cohen's kappa with its limitation note, the confusion matrix, each reviewer's prevalence,
the disagreement count and the number of blanks. Writes `reviewer_agreement.json`,
`disagreements.csv` and a blank `adjudication_template.csv`.

It **does not read the evaluator key**. Fix any reported missing or invalid value by asking that
reviewer — never by guessing — then re-run.

## Step 9 — third-party adjudication

Copy `adjudication_template.csv` to `adjudication_filled.csv` and give it to a third person who sees
both reviewers' values and rationales **but not the evaluator's label and not the blinding map**. If
your supervisor is reviewer B, the adjudicator must be someone else.

The disagreement list they receive carries the episode id, the field, both judgements and the task
type only: the system, prompt and run stay hidden from the adjudicator too. For each row they fill
`final_adjudicated_judgement` (an allowed value for that field),
`adjudicator_rationale` (one or two sentences) and `resolution_category` (`Evaluator logic bug`,
`Policy configuration issue`, `Annotation guide ambiguity`, `Human annotation error`, `Genuine
unresolved ambiguity`). The script never writes into these columns. The adjudicated values, plus the
fields both reviewers already agreed on, form the **human reference standard**.

## Step 10 — open the evaluator key, and only now

Bring the key back from `~/medagent_sealed/` (or pass its path). This is the first point at which
the evaluator's labels enter the exercise, and it comes **after** adjudication is complete.

## Step 11 — evaluator versus adjudicated human labels

```bash
python scripts/analyze_human_annotations.py \
  --reviewer-a <reviewer A sheet> --reviewer-b <reviewer B sheet> \
  --adjudicated outputs/human_validation_v1/adjudication_filled.csv \
  --compare-evaluator \
  --key ~/medagent_sealed/EVALUATOR_KEY_do_not_open_before_annotating.csv \
  --scored outputs/heldout_v1_audit \
  --mapping ~/medagent_sealed/blinding_map_do_not_share.csv \
  --out outputs/human_validation_v1
```

`--mapping` is what finally joins the hidden system, prompt, run and case for each episode; it is
accepted **only** in this stage, and it writes `disagreements_with_mapping.csv` plus
disagreement counts per model and per prompt. The command refuses without `--adjudicated`, and refuses while any mandatory annotation is missing
or invalid. It reports exact agreement, a confusion matrix (human rows, evaluator columns),
precision/recall/F1 per category where defined, a per-task breakdown, a per-required-action
breakdown and the counts per resolution category, into `evaluator_vs_human.json`. `--scored` is
needed only for the turn-level fields (`h_premature_action`, `h_unsafe_action`), which the key does
not carry; without it they are reported as "not comparable" rather than silently skipped.

Report reviewer-versus-reviewer agreement and evaluator-versus-human agreement as **two separate
results**. The second is only interpretable in the light of the first: if two careful humans disagree
often on a label, the evaluator cannot be held to a higher standard. This is also the first point at
which results may be broken down by system or configuration, because that is when the mapping is
joined.

## Step 12 — turn confirmed defects into regression tests

For every disagreement adjudicated as `Evaluator logic bug` or `Policy configuration issue`:

1. Write a failing test in `tests/test_v2_action_requirements.py` from that episode's trajectory,
   naming the `ANN-nn` id and the adjudicated label in a comment.
2. Fix the code or the policy.
3. Re-run the full suite, re-score the affected runs, and record what changed in `DECISIONS.md`,
   adding corrected figures as a new dated section rather than editing published tables.

This is how the audit of 2026-11-17 handled its three bugs, and it is the only route by which a
human annotation may change the evaluator.

## Scope statement for the thesis

If no qualified clinical reviewer takes part, describe the exercise as **research-team annotation of
benchmark compliance**: whether the evaluator's labels can be reproduced by careful human reading of
the same trajectory against the benchmark task's own instructions. It is **not** clinical validation.
High agreement means the labels are well defined and reproducible; it says nothing about whether the
benchmark's thresholds, dose rules or the synthetic cases are clinically sound. Those remain open in
`medagentbench_setup/heldout_v1_clinical_review.md`.
